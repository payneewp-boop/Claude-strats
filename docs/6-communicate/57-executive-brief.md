# 57 · Executive Brief

**Phase:** 6 — Communicate · **Use when:** you have one page and four minutes of attention

## What it does

Compresses a body of work into a single page an executive can read in four minutes and act
on — the answer, the reasoning, the ask, and the consequence of inaction. Compression is the
work; the discipline is deciding what does not appear.

## Inputs you need

- The full analysis
- The specific executive or forum, and what they care about
- The decision or action required
- What they already know, so you do not spend the page on context

## Prompt

```
You are writing a one-page executive brief on [SUBJECT] for [EXECUTIVE / FORUM].

One page. Assume four minutes of attention, possibly interrupted. Assume they read the
first two lines and the last two lines regardless of what is in between.

Structure:

HEADLINE (one sentence)
The single most important thing. If they read nothing else, this.

THE ASK (two sentences)
What you need from them, specifically: a decision, a resource, an intervention, or nothing
at all. If it is nothing, say "for information" so they can stop reading with confidence.

SITUATION (3–4 sentences)
Where things stand, in numbers. Only what they need for the decision — do not rehearse
what they already know.

THE ANALYSIS (3–5 bullets)
The findings that drive the recommendation, each one line, each with its number. Ordered by
importance. Not a summary of the work — the conclusions of it.

RECOMMENDATION (2–3 sentences)
What to do, what it costs, what it returns, over what period.

RISKS (2–3 bullets)
The material risks and what is being done about each. One line each. Include the maximum
downside — its absence is noticed by anyone experienced.

IF WE DO NOTHING (one or two sentences)
The quantified cost of the status quo.

NEXT STEPS (3 bullets)
Action, owner, date. Shows the work is already moving.

Then produce:

WHAT I LEFT OUT
List what did not make the page and why, so the author can judge the compression. Flag
anything omitted that the executive might specifically ask about, so they are ready for it.

THE THREE QUESTIONS THEY WILL ASK
Based on what this executive cares about, and the one-line answer to each.

Rules:
- One page means one page. If it does not fit, cut analysis, not the ask or the risks.
- Every claim carries its number. Adjectives without numbers are noise at this altitude.
- No consultant vocabulary. Write it as you would say it.
- Do not bury the cost, the risk, or the ask below the fold.

MATERIAL:
[PASTE FULL ANALYSIS, AUDIENCE CONTEXT, DECISION REQUIRED, WHAT THEY ALREADY KNOW]
```

## Output you should get

A genuine one-pager in fixed sections, plus a compression audit and three anticipated
questions with answers.

## Quality bar

- **"What I left out" is the author's safety net.** Executives ask about the omitted thing
  more often than about the included thing.
- **Check the headline alone.** If it does not carry the message by itself, rewrite it.
- **Reject** any brief where the ask appears below the analysis. Executives read top-down and
  stop early.
